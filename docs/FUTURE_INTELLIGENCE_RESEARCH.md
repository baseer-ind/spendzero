# Future Intelligence — Research Library

Verified sources behind Future Intelligence lessons. Findings are recorded as
the studies state them, with limitations and the wording Project Future may and
may not use. Encoded in `webapp/src/lib/learn.ts` (`RESEARCH`).

## 1. Krishna, Briesch, Lehmann & Yuan (2002)
- **Title:** A Meta-analysis of the Impact of Price Presentation on Perceived Savings
- **Publication:** Journal of Retailing
- **Link:** https://business.columbia.edu/faculty/research/meta-analysis-impact-price-presentation-perceived-savings
- **Methodology:** Meta-analysis of 20 published studies.
- **Finding:** How a promotion is presented affects perceived savings; presenting
  a regular/reference price can increase the perceived value of deals.
- **Limitation:** Aggregates prior work; effects vary by context and are largely
  about *perceived* (not actual) savings.
- **Safe wording:** "Research suggests a reference price can raise how valuable a
  deal feels."
- **Prohibited:** "Discounts make everyone buy." / any dopamine claim.
- **Feature inspired:** The Discount Trap lesson; the Decision Moment showing real
  amount vs. future.

## 2. Chen, Monroe & Lou (1998)
- **Title:** The Effects of Framing Price Promotion Messages on Consumers' Perceptions and Purchase Intentions
- **Publication:** Journal of Retailing
- **Link:** https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022435999801006
- **Methodology:** Experimental study of percentage vs. absolute framing.
- **Finding:** Framing the same reduction as a percentage vs. an amount changed
  its perceived significance, depending on the product's price level.
- **Limitation:** Depends on price level/product; measured perceptions and intent.
- **Safe wording:** "Studies have found the framing of the same discount can
  change how big it feels."
- **Prohibited:** Claiming a fixed universal effect size.
- **Feature inspired:** Discount Trap; future "percentage vs ₹ off" lesson.

## 3. Bayer & Ke (2013)
- **Title:** Discounts and Consumer Search Behavior: The Role of Framing
- **Publication:** Journal of Economic Psychology
- **Link:** https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0167487013001037
- **Methodology:** Experiment with objectively identical shopping problems framed
  as discounts vs. not.
- **Finding:** People searched less when prices were framed as discounts, relying
  on salient promotion features rather than the underlying price.
- **Limitation:** Controlled experiment; may differ from real marketplaces.
- **Safe wording:** "In this experiment, discount framing changed behaviour even
  when the underlying problem was identical."
- **Prohibited:** "Discounts switch off your brain."
- **Feature inspired:** The pause — slowing the decision so the underlying price
  gets attention.

## 4. Wu et al. (2020)
- **Title:** How does scarcity promotion lead to impulse purchase in the online market? A field experiment
- **Publication:** Journal of Business Research
- **Link:** https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0378720619302095
- **Methodology:** Field experiment on scarcity promotions online.
- **Finding:** Limited-quantity and limited-time promotions can contribute to
  impulse-purchase behaviour.
- **Limitation:** Specific platforms/products; "contribute to" ≠ "cause for
  everyone".
- **Safe wording:** "Scarcity promotions can contribute to impulse purchasing."
- **Prohibited:** "Timers force you to buy."
- **Feature inspired:** The Scarcity Clock lesson.

## India-specific evidence (market & academic)

These support Project Future's direction (convenience, offers and personalisation
drive online shopping in India). **Label honestly:** items 5–8 are industry/market
surveys (Ipsos, PwC, YouGov via Statista), not peer-reviewed experiments; items 9–10
are Indian academic studies. Report figures as "a survey found," not as universal
laws, and do not imply we measured them ourselves.

## 5. Ipsos — India Shopper / "Clicking Future" (industry survey)
- **Finding:** 68% cited speed/convenience and 61% cited offers/discounts as
  motivations for online shopping in India; online shopping is strongly
  app-oriented.
- **Link:** https://www.ipsos.com/en-in/ipsos-shopper-insights-unveils-clicking-future-trends-driving-indias-online-shopping-surge-new
- **Safe wording:** "An Ipsos India survey found most online shoppers cited
  convenience and offers as motivations."
- **Limitation:** Market survey; self-reported; methodology differs from controlled studies.

## 6. PwC — Global Consumer Insights, India (industry survey)
- **Finding:** ~66% frequently/almost always use smartphones to research products
  for prices, offers and availability; ~50% use retailer apps to research/compare.
- **Link:** https://www.pwc.in/industries/retail-and-consumer/global-consumer-insights-pulse-survey-india-perspective.html
- **Safe wording:** "PwC's India consumer research found smartphones and retailer
  apps are widely used to research prices and offers."
- **Limitation:** Market survey; self-reported.

## 7. YouGov India 2024 (industry survey, via Statista)
- **Finding:** Convenience and user-friendly apps are major aspects of online
  shopping behaviour; shopping is strongly app-oriented in India.
- **Link:** https://www.statista.com/statistics/1465566/india-preferred-online-shopping-platform-by-consumers/
- **Safe wording:** "Surveys report Indian online shopping is strongly app-based."
- **Limitation:** Market data; definitions vary by report.

## 8. Indian retail/e-commerce impulse-buying study (2024/2025)
- **Finding:** Examining physical retail, e-commerce and mobile apps, emotional/
  situational factors matter in impulse buying; personalization, incentives and
  streamlined checkout can stimulate impulse purchases.
- **Link:** https://indianjournals.com/article/eaj-69-2-034
- **Safe wording:** "An Indian study found personalization, incentives and quick
  checkout can contribute to impulse buying."
- **Limitation:** Single study; context-specific; 'can stimulate' ≠ 'always causes'.

## 9. Indian mobile-app shoppers study (2024, n≈302)
- **Finding:** Personalization, contextual offers, privacy/security and retailer
  reputation were associated with smartphone-shopping engagement; app incentives
  interacted with shopping intentions and purchase frequency.
- **Link:** https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/pdf/10.1111/ijcs.12992
- **Safe wording:** "A study of Indian app shoppers associated personalization and
  incentives with higher engagement."
- **Limitation:** Correlational; sample of 302; associations, not proven causes.

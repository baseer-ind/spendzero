# Spending Behaviour Assessment & Profile

> Short (~8–10 items, 60–90s), evidence-informed, **non-judgmental, non-clinical**. It produces a
> warm, strengths-based profile — never a grade or a diagnosis. It exists to tailor the experience
> and to make the user feel understood.

## Design principles
- Plain language, first-person, no jargon.
- 5-point agreement scale (Strongly disagree → Strongly agree) unless noted.
- Balance affective and cognitive impulse items (per Verplanken & Herabadi, 2001, who separate
  *cognitive* and *affective* impulse-buying tendencies) and include trigger/context items.
- Conceptually informed by validated instruments (Rook & Fisher, 1995, Buying Impulsiveness Scale;
  Verplanken & Herabadi, 2001). **We do not reproduce copyrighted scale items verbatim** — we write
  original items measuring the same constructs.
- Store answers locally (and later per account); allow retake; show change over time (growth, framed positively).

## Dimensions measured
1. **Affective impulse** — buying to chase a feeling / excitement.
2. **Cognitive deliberation** (reverse) — do they plan vs. act in the moment.
3. **Trigger: emotion** — spending when stressed/sad/bored.
4. **Trigger: social/scroll** — spending prompted by feeds, sales, FOMO, late-night browsing.
5. **Present bias** — future goals feel abstract vs. the now.
6. **Post-purchase regret** — how often purchases are regretted (a motivation signal, handled gently).

## Example items (original wording)
1. When I see something I like, I often buy it without much thought. *(affective)*
2. I usually think over a purchase for a while before deciding. *(deliberation, reverse)*
3. I shop or browse to lift my mood when I'm stressed, bored, or down. *(emotion trigger)*
4. Sales, app notifications, or scrolling often lead me to buy things I didn't plan to. *(social/scroll)*
5. Late at night I'm more likely to add things to a cart. *(scroll/context)*
6. Saving for something far away feels less real than buying something now. *(present bias)*
7. I often regret small purchases a day or two later. *(regret)*
8. I have clear things I'm saving toward. *(goal clarity, reverse of present bias)*
9. I can usually walk away from something I wanted once the moment passes. *(self-efficacy, strength)*
10. Spending money on the right things genuinely makes my life better. *(values — ensures non-shaming framing)*

> Item 10 is deliberate: it affirms that *good* spending exists, keeping the whole assessment non-judgmental.

## Scoring (simple, transparent)
- Normalise each item 0–4; reverse where noted. Average into the six dimensions (0–1).
- Derive an **Impulse index** (affective + triggers − deliberation − self-efficacy, clamped 0–1) and a
  **Goal-clarity index**.
- Map to a **primary profile** by the highest-loading trigger, tempered by impulse/goal-clarity.
- Never show a single "spending score." Show dimension bars + the narrative profile.

## Profiles (strengths-based, non-judgmental)
Each profile returns: a one-line identity, *what tends to trigger unnecessary spending for you*, a
*genuine strength*, and *how Project Future will help you specifically*.

| Profile | Signature | Strength we name | How the app tailors |
|---------|-----------|------------------|---------------------|
| **The Spark** | High affective impulse; buys on excitement | Enthusiastic, decisive | Strong "Pause" beat + instant future trade-off before checkout |
| **The Soother** | Emotion-triggered spending | Emotionally honest, self-aware | Mood-aware check-ins; non-judgmental reframes; alternative wins |
| **The Scroller** | Feed/sale/late-night prompted | Curious, discovers great things | Evening nudge; "you were browsing — here's your future" framing |
| **The Planner** | Low impulse, plans well | Disciplined, consistent | Fewer interruptions; optimisation + bigger/longer goals, B2B-grade insights |
| **The Dreamer** | Low goal clarity, future feels abstract | Open, aspirational | Heavy future-self work; concrete goal-setting first |

(A user is shown one primary profile; secondary traits appear as dimension bars. Profiles are a lens,
not a box — copy says "right now" and invites retakes.)

## Output screen copy (tone)
- "You're **The Scroller**. Nothing wrong with that — you find genuinely good things. The leak is
  the late-night, half-attention adds. Here's how we'll have your back…"
- Always: strength first, trigger second, help third. Never "you have a problem."

## Data & privacy
- Answers are behavioural preferences, not special-category data, but treat them as sensitive.
- Stored locally today; with an account, encrypted in transit; used only to personalise (disclosed in
  the data-safety form). Never sold. (See MONETISATION_ARCHITECTURE.md for the hard lines.)

# Behavioural Foundation — Project Future

> Evidence-informed, not clinical. We cite established literature and describe findings
> **qualitatively**. We do **not** publish invented "dopamine" statistics, and the app must
> **not** make neuroscience or medical claims. Any specific figure used in marketing must be
> verified against the primary source first, and framed as the researchers framed it.

## 1. The urge peaks *before* the purchase, then fades
- **Impulse buying is cue-driven and affect-laden.** Rook, D. (1987), "The Buying Impulse,"
  *Journal of Consumer Research*. Beatty & Ferrell (1998) on in-store browsing and impulse.
- **Self-regulation is depletable**, so impulse buying rises when willpower is low (tired, stressed,
  decision-fatigued). Vohs, K. & Faber, R. (2007), "Spent Resources," *Journal of Consumer Research*.
- **Design implication:** the single most valuable intervention is an intentional **Pause** between
  urge and action. A short, calm delay lets the affective spike subside before the decision.

## 2. A small, chosen delay / precommitment reduces impulsive spending
- **Precommitment and self-imposed deadlines improve self-control.** Ariely, D. & Wertenbroch, K.
  (2002), "Procrastination, Deadlines, and Performance," *Psychological Science*.
- **Design implication:** the Pause + "Decide" step is a voluntary precommitment device, not a lecture.

## 3. Present bias makes the future feel cheap
- **People over-weight the present** (hyperbolic / quasi-hyperbolic discounting). Laibson, D. (1997),
  "Golden Eggs and Hyperbolic Discounting," *Quarterly Journal of Economics*; O'Donoghue, T. & Rabin, M.
  (1999), "Doing It Now or Later," *American Economic Review*.
- **Design implication:** make the future concrete and *present* — a named goal, a progress bar that
  moves today, "X closer now." Translate money into the future thing, immediately.

## 4. Making the future self vivid increases saving
- **Hershfield, H. et al. (2011),** "Increasing Saving Behavior Through Age-Progressed Renderings of the
  Future Self," *Journal of Marketing Research*: people who engaged with a vivid future self allocated
  more to long-term savings.
- **Design implication:** the "letter from your future self" and the goal imagery are not decoration —
  they are the mechanism. Keep them personal and specific.

## 5. Anticipation drives wanting; price can trigger "stop"
- **Anticipated reward precedes action.** Reward-prediction-error work (Schultz, W., 1997, *Science*)
  and anticipatory-affect work (Knutson & Greer, 2008). Describe as "anticipation of reward," not with
  fabricated numbers.
- **Neural predictors of purchasing.** Knutson, Rick, Wimmer, Prelec & Loewenstein (2007), "Neural
  Predictors of Purchases," *Neuron*: anticipatory reward signals were associated with buying, while
  activation linked to the *price/"pain of paying"* was associated with **not** buying.
- **Pain of paying.** Prelec, D. & Loewenstein, G. (1998), "The Red and the Black," *Marketing Science*.
- **Design implication:** surface the trade-off *before* purchase (what this costs your future), which
  recruits the "stop" response, without shaming.

## 6. Earmarking money to a goal changes how it's treated
- **Mental accounting.** Thaler, R. (1985) "Mental Accounting and Consumer Choice," *Marketing Science*;
  Thaler (1999) "Mental Accounting Matters."
- **Low-friction future commitment.** Thaler, R. & Benartzi, S. (2004), "Save More Tomorrow," *Journal
  of Political Economy*.
- **Design implication:** a dedicated Future Fund per goal; redirected money is "already spoken for."

## 7. Motivation rises as the goal gets closer
- **Goal-gradient effect.** Kivetz, Urminsky & Zheng (2006), "The Goal-Gradient Hypothesis Resurrected,"
  *Journal of Marketing Research*.
- **Fresh-start effect.** Dai, Milkman & Riis (2014), "The Fresh Start Effect," *Management Science*.
- **Design implication:** always show proximity to the goal; offer fresh starts (new month/goal) that
  preserve savings but reset momentum.

## 8. Avoid the traps
- **No shaming / no "all spending is bad."** Framing matters (Tversky & Kahneman, 1981, framing effects).
- **Guard against moral licensing** — "I saved, so now I can splurge." Khan & Dhar (2006), "Licensing
  Effect in Consumer Choice," *JMR*. Celebrate consistency, not single acts that invite a rebound.
- **Streaks must forgive.** Punishing streaks increase drop-off; design forgiving streaks.
- **No dark patterns.** Reward anticipation is used to support the user's *own* stated goal, never to
  drive compulsive app use.

## Claims policy (for marketing & in-app copy)
- Qualitative statements grounded in the above are fine ("the urge to buy usually fades if you wait").
- **Do not** state invented percentages, "dopamine hits," or brain claims.
- Any figure cited externally must link to the primary source and match its framing.
- The app is a behaviour-support tool, **not** a medical, clinical, or financial-advice product.

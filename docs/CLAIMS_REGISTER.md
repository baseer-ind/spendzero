# Claims Register — Project Future

Every behavioural claim used in-product must appear here with its source, what the source actually
supports, approved wording, and prohibited wording. No invented stats. No medical/addiction claims.

| # | In-product claim (approved) | Source | What the source supports | Prohibited wording |
|---|------------------------------|--------|--------------------------|--------------------|
| 1 | "The urge to buy is loudest before you buy — and it fades fast." | Rook (1987), *JCR*; Beatty & Ferrell (1998) | Impulse buying is cue-driven and affect-laden; urges are momentary | "Scientists proved cravings are an addiction" |
| 2 | "A short pause usually makes the choice clearer." | Ariely & Wertenbroch (2002), *Psych Science*; Vohs & Faber (2007), *JCR* | Precommitment/delay improves self-control; impulse buying rises when self-regulation is low | Any specific % reduction figure |
| 3 | "Saving for something far away can feel less real than buying now." | Laibson (1997), *QJE*; O'Donoghue & Rabin (1999), *AER* | Present bias / hyperbolic discounting | "Your brain is wired to be broke" |
| 4 | "Making your future vivid helps you choose it." | Hershfield et al. (2011), *JMR* | Engaging a vivid future self increased savings allocation | Claiming guaranteed savings outcomes |
| 5 | "Seeing the trade-off before you buy helps you decide." | Knutson et al. (2007), *Neuron*; Prelec & Loewenstein (1998), *Marketing Science* | Anticipatory reward linked to buying; price/"pain of paying" linked to not buying | "Dopamine makes you shop" / any dopamine statistic |
| 6 | "Progress feels better as your goal gets closer." | Kivetz, Urminsky & Zheng (2006), *JMR* | Goal-gradient effect | — |
| 7 | "A fresh start is a good time to build momentum." | Dai, Milkman & Riis (2014), *Management Science* | Fresh-start effect | — |
| 8 | "Spending on the right things genuinely improves life." (non-shaming stance) | Framing literature (Tversky & Kahneman, 1981); guard against moral licensing (Khan & Dhar, 2006) | Framing matters; licensing can trigger rebound spending | "All spending is bad" / shaming language |

## Global rules
- Qualitative only. No fabricated percentages or "dopamine hits."
- Never claim to treat addiction, mental illness, or financial disorders.
- Never present academic findings as medical or guaranteed-outcome claims.
- Any externally published figure must link to the primary source and match its framing.
